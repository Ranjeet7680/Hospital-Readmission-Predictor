How does HRP calculate the 30-day unplanned readmission probability?
expand_more

The Hospital Readmission Predictor utilizes an ensemble gradient-boosted decision tree architecture (XGBoost) trained and externally validated across 30,000 multi-center inpatient electronic health records.

The model evaluates 28 multi-modal feature vectors, including:

  • Physiological Lab Biomarkers: Serum creatinine (>1.5 mg/dL), hemoglobin (<10.5 g/dL), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), and HbA1c elevation.
  • Inpatient Utilization History: Prior admissions within 12 months, emergency department visits, and current length of stay.
  • Comorbidity Indices: Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI) weighting for CHF, COPD, Diabetes, Chronic Kidney Disease, and hypertension.
  • Pharmacological Polypharmacy: Active medication counts (>8 prescription regimens) and high-risk anticoagulant titrations.
Predicted Probability: P(Y=1) = σ( Σ [ f_k(x) ] ) where ROC-AUC = 0.9794, PR-AUC = 0.9621, Brier Score = 0.082
What clinical intervention protocols are mandated for High-Risk (>60%) patients?
expand_more

When a patient's risk index exceeds the critical 60% threshold, the clinical decision support system automatically fires a CMS HRRP Compliance Directive. Hospital clinical teams must confirm:

  1. 72-Hour Primary Care Telehealth / In-Person Visit: Appointment confirmed on the discharge summary with the designated physician before discharge order clearance.
  2. Pharmacist-Led Medication Therapy Reconciliation: Clinical pharmacist audits active prescriptions to eliminate duplicate sedatives, optimize beta-blockers, and reconcile anticoagulants.
  3. Transition of Care (ToC) Coordinator Assignment: Care coordinator contacts the patient within 48 hours post-discharge to confirm transportation, medication adherence, and vital signs.
  4. Remote Biometric Monitoring: Order for cellular-connected blood pressure cuff and pulse oximeter for CHF and COPD cohorts.
How do I interpret the TreeSHAP Waterfall Explanation on the patient assessment card?
expand_more

TreeSHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) provides mathematical attribution for every clinical feature contributing to that individual patient's score:

  • Red Bars (+ SHAP Value): Factors that elevated the readmission likelihood (e.g. +14% due to serum creatinine = 2.4 mg/dL, +11% due to 3 prior emergency admissions).
  • Green Bars (- SHAP Value): Protective factors mitigating readmission (e.g. -8% due to active post-discharge nurse visit enrollment, -6% due to stable blood glucose).
  • Base Value: The baseline 20.4% hospital average readmission rate from which the individual patient's risk is calculated.
Is confidential medical history exposed on the Digital Health ID QR code?
expand_more

No. The QR code does not contain unencrypted diagnostic text, lab results, or full clinical notes.

Instead, it encodes a cryptographically signed verification payload (AES-256-GCM + HMAC-SHA256) containing the patient's anonymous ID token, credential expiration timestamp, and authorized issuing hospital facility key. When scanned by paramedics or triage clinics, it verifies authentic enrollment without risking data leaks.

How does the QR verification work in emergency ambulances without internet?
expand_more

The built-in QR Scanner (`/qr/scanner`) utilizes a client-side WebAssembly decoder paired with local public-key cryptography. Even during zero-connectivity field conditions, the scanner verifies the digital signature against pre-cached hospital keys to confirm identity, primary diagnosis class, and emergency contact details.

How do we connect HRP Clinical with Epic or Cerner via HL7 FHIR v4.0?
expand_more

HRP exposes standardized HL7 CDS Hooks v1.0 endpoints located at `/cds-services/patient-readmission-risk`.

When an inpatient chart is opened in Epic Hyperspace or Cerner PowerChart, the EHR fires a `patient-view` hook. HRP analyzes the incoming FHIR Patient, Condition, and Observation resources and returns actionable SMART decision support cards with the calculated risk tier and ordered protocol links.

What security and compliance frameworks protect patient data?
expand_more

HRP Clinical adheres to HIPAA Security Rule (45 CFR Part 160 and Part 164):

  • Encryption at Rest & Transit: All databases, credentials, and telemetry streams use AES-256-GCM and TLS 1.3.
  • Cryptographic Audit Trails: Every chart access, risk computation, and export generates an immutable HMAC audit record viewable in the Administration Console.
  • Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): Multi-factor authentication with granular permissions across Doctors, Nurses, Coordinators, and Patients.

What is the CMS Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program (HRRP) and how does HRP prevent fines?
expand_more

Under Section 3025 of the Affordable Care Act, CMS assesses up to a 3.0% penalty across ALL inpatient Medicare payments for hospitals exhibiting excess 30-day readmissions in six designated clinical conditions (AMI, Heart Failure, Pneumonia, COPD, CABG, and Elective Arthroplasty).

HRP Clinical helps hospitals avoid multi-million dollar penalties by flagging vulnerable discharge candidates 48 to 72 hours prior to bed clearance, triggering proactive care transition bundles.

What is "Break-Glass" emergency access and when should clinicians use it?
expand_more

In acute life-threatening situations where a patient is unable to grant consent or records are restricted, attending physicians can invoke Break-Glass Emergency Access from the login/patient banner. This temporarily lifts permission locks and flags the session in the high-priority hospital security audit queue for retrospective compliance review.

Which laboratory biomarker thresholds carry the heaviest readmission weights?
expand_more

Clinical analysis demonstrates the highest feature importances reside in:

  • Serum Creatinine > 1.6 mg/dL: Strongest single indicator of acute kidney injury and cardiac decompensation.
  • Hemoglobin < 9.5 g/dL: Inpatient anemia correlates with increased 30-day oxygenation failure.
  • BUN/Creatinine Ratio > 20: Prerenal azotemia and hypovolemia.
  • HbA1c > 9.0%: Uncontrolled metabolic glycemic variability causing vascular delays.
